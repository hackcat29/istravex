## What it flags

Two patterns:

1. **`TransactionEnvelope.Fee()` or `FeeBumpFee()`** in a function that never reads `FeeCharged`. The envelope fee is the bid, not the charge.
2. **`TransactionEnvelope.SourceAccount()` in fee logic** (the function's name or body mentions fees) that never checks for a fee-bump (`IsFeeBump`, `FeeBumpAccount`, `FeeAccount`…). On a fee-bump, `SourceAccount()` is the inner source, who didn't pay.

## Why it matters

On the [fee-bump fixture](/corpus/fixtures#fee-bump-fee-source), reading fees from the envelope gives the wrong account *and* the wrong amount: 46,341 stroops charged to `GDMV…COIW`, when the ledger charged 40,354 to `GA2J…HVAW`. The [naive replay indexer](/tools/replay) makes exactly this mistake.

## Fix

```go
payer := env.SourceAccount()
if env.IsFeeBump() {
    payer = env.FeeBumpAccount()
}
charged := result.Result.FeeCharged
```

## Why it's limited to fee logic

`SourceAccount()` is often right. An operation without its own source runs as the inner transaction's source, fee-bump or not, so using it as the fallback is correct. The first version of this rule flagged every call and produced two false positives in wallet-backend, both correct fallbacks. Restricting it to fee logic removed them without losing the real pattern.
