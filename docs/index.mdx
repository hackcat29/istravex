A Stellar transaction can be perfectly valid and still be misread by the software that consumes it.

Here is a real one. On 14 November 2026, mainnet ledger 65,474,279 recorded a USDC payment to the muxed account ID `1764988090084626463`. Every SDK decodes it correctly. Then a JavaScript service passes that ID through `Number()` on its way to the database, and what gets stored is `1764988090084626400`. Nothing errors and nothing logs. The payment is now credited to a customer who doesn't exist.

Istravex exists to catch that class of bug before it ships. It's a shared, versioned corpus of rare-but-valid Stellar XDR, plus the tools to run your SDK or indexer against it and see exactly where it disagrees with the ledger.

## Who it's for

- **SDK maintainers** who want proof that decode, re-encode and native conversions survive real ledger data, and who want a regression test that other SDKs share.
- **Indexer authors** (explorers, wallets, analytics, custody) who want to know whether their pipeline stores the right records for the right accounts.
- **Reviewers and auditors** who need evidence, not assurances: every result links back to a fixture, a ledger and a commit.
